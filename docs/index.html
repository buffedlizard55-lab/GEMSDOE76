The metric is a budget, not a segmentation score

[OFFICIAL] DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw) with triangular kernel k(d)=max(1−d/300m,0), α=0.2 FP, β=0.8 FN, R=3 px. Problem description § Performance metric · Tversky index

Writing T=TPw, S=Σp, M=Σ p·maxk, K=|G| gives [DERIVED, verified in tests/test_metric.py vs brute-force O(N²)]:

DTI = T / (0.2·(T+S−M) + 0.8·K)      (★)

Marginal rule [DERIVED]: adding one unit mass with realised kernel weight k raises DTI iff k > 0.2·DTI (at 0.2778 bar = 0.0556; at 0.3262 leader bar = 0.0652). Verified by differentiation of (★): denominator rises by exactly α regardless of location.

Geothermal vent science — why these faults matter

Great Basin geothermal systems are fault-controlled: permeability is created by dilatant jogs, stepovers, and transtensional nodes where crust thins. Hidden faults under Quaternary alluvium are the exploration target — they have no surface scarp but host upflow via fracture permeability and clay-cap breach.

  • Walker Lane shear (NW Nevada): dextral shear + Basin-and-Range extension → releasing bends generate geothermal upwelling (Faulds & Hinz model). Hidden faults in Monte Cristo area ruptured 2020 but were unmapped — proof that active faults exist without scarp preservation.
  • MT conductivity: clay alteration cap (smectite) is conductive; a breach in the conductive layer marks upflow conduit — MT cond_surf step is direct geothermal indicator.
  • Radiometric alteration: K leaching / U enrichment halos around geothermal outflow; detected by airborne gamma spectrometry (GeoDAWN radiometric survey). GeoDAWN carries K, Th, U channels at 400 m line spacing, 80 m terrain clearance — official free source.
  • Geodetic strain: INGENIOUS GPS shows second invariant 19.9 nstrain/yr median, shear 9.7, dilatant 5.5 mm/yr — opening is measurable where faults creep.
