Why the incumbent scored 0.2778 — and why this lane could not beat it

Measured: the incumbent h33-h33-2-b2…-zeros is a strict subset of the earlier 44,090-dot "D2.8" raster — the supervised H19-5 surface thinned to isolated dots, with the 6,436 dots within 200 m of the catalogue deleted (37,654 dots left). The -zeros vs -nan twins are bit-identical inside the footprint (verified); "zeros" (0.0 outside instead of NaN) only guarantees passing the portal's whole-raster [0,1] check — it adds no score. Three metric facts do the work: β/α = 4 makes recall cheap (marginal bar k > 0.056 at s=0.2778); the 300 m max-kernel makes isolated dots nearly as good as lines at ~⅓ the FP cost; and catalogue pixels are masked in scoring, so near-catalogue dots are pure FP against expert "new fault" truth — deleting them was free.

Why G76 failed: the 1 m-LiDAR dipole bands, max-pooled to 100 m, are saturated by every fan channel and gully (best single-field AUC 0.631; fitted holdout AUC 0.620). Concentrated but imprecise dots lose to a uniform spread under this max-cover metric. Per-pixel 100 m fields cap at AUC ≈ 0.63 across every stack we measured (LiDAR, DEM10, GeoDAWN) — the winning ingredient is learned multi-physics + spatial context, which is the incumbent family's recipe, not a new transform. Full analysis: docs/research/why-02778.md.
