# Why did `h33-h33-2-b2-…-zeros` (0.2778, user-reported) top the family — and can it be beaten?

**Short answer.** The file is not a new model. Measured byte-for-byte this session, it is a
*strict subset* of the earlier "D2.8" dotted raster: the H19-5 supervised multi-physics surface,
thinned to 44,090 isolated dots, with the 6,436 dots lying within 2 px (200 m) of the
USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue deleted, leaving 37,654 binary dots. The `-zeros` suffix (0.0 outside
the scored footprint instead of NaN) contributes **nothing** to the score — the in-footprint
values of the `-zeros` and `-nan` twins are bit-identical (verified) — but it is the encoding
that cannot fail the portal's whole-raster "[0, 1]" range check, which is almost certainly why
the *zeros* twin is the one that was successfully submitted and scored. The score itself comes
from three compounding mechanisms, each verified below. **Beating it requires a better
probability surface, not better emission cosmetics** — and this session's pre-registered attempt
(Lane G76) failed that test decisively, which is itself the most useful measurement in this repo.

Everything below is labelled **[MEASURED]** (this session, from local bytes),
**[OFFICIAL]** (organizer sources), **[USER-REPORTED]** (the prompt's score list; no organizer
receipt exists in any cloned repo — IR-76-03), or **[INFERENCE]**.

## 1. The metric makes "dotted, recall-heavy, catalogue-free" the right shape

**[OFFICIAL]** DrivenData competition 306 scores a distance-weighted Tversky index with a
triangular kernel k(d)=max(1−d/300 m, 0), α=0.2 (false positives), β=0.8 (false negatives):
TP_w sums, over each truth pixel, the *maximum* of p·k over predictions within 300 m; FP_w sums
p·(1−max k) over predicted pixels. Known USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue pixels are masked out of
scoring entirely (official forum thread 11516). Three consequences:

1. **Recall is worth 4× precision** (β/α). Exact marginal algebra: one more unit dot with
   realised kernel credit *k* raises the score iff k > α·s — at s=0.2778 the bar is just
   **0.056**. Cheap coverage wins until a dot's expected credit is nearly zero.
2. **Dots beat lines.** Because TP credit is a *maximum* over a 300 m neighbourhood, isolated
   dots spaced ~300 m apart harvest nearly the same TP as a continuous trace while paying FP on
   ~1/3 of the pixels. **[MEASURED]** Every top-family raster is binary isolated dots with zero
   8-neighbour adjacencies (H33-B2, D2.8, Anderson: 0.000).
3. **Mass on/near the catalogue is wasted or worse.** On-catalogue pixels are masked (no TP, no
   FP — pure waste). Pixels 100–200 m off a catalogue trace are scored, but the hidden test
   faults are *new* structures labelled by experts precisely because they are not in the
   catalogue; a dot hugging a known fault is overwhelmingly likely to be tracing that fault's
   positional error and pays full FP against the hidden truth.

## 3. Why the H19-5 surface itself wins live

**[MEASURED from 19GEMSDOE source]** H19-5 is a 4-quadrant out-of-fold supervised ensemble over
four corroborating "lines" (openess/LRM scarp terms dominant; power-law tip population; backward
thermal/geochemical orphans; geopotential worms), gated by the second-best line (single-layer
patterns are discounted), quantile-matched across the LiDAR coverage boundary, and — critically —
trained with **zero distance-to-catalogue features**, so it emits far from known faults
(**[MEASURED]** 69.8% of H33-B2 dots are >1 km from the catalogue; median ≈ 2.0 km). It behaves
like what it is approximating: an expert mapper working from multi-sensor evidence. Every lane
that instead optimised catalogue recovery (supervised models with catalogue-distance context,
SGMC-direct emission, basement steps, Euler clusters…) scored ≤0.19 live **[USER-REPORTED list]**,
and this session's audit shows why the offline instruments kept misleading those efforts
(§5).

## 4. Why *this* session's lane failed — the informative part

Lane G76 tested whether the *measurement* experts use (a youthful scarp's crest-toe Laplacian
dipole with slope-facing polarity in 1 m LiDAR derivatives) could replace the learned ensemble.
Pre-registered, two experiments, shared evaluator (`gems57-pooled-hide-v2`, whole-component
hide-and-recover, n=11,321 withheld px):

- **HOLDOUT-DTI**: Exp 1 (hand-set dipole product) 0.0217 [0.0137, 0.0303]; Exp 2 (logistic
  combiner over the same 10 components) **0.0218 [0.0145, 0.0303]**; fair-H33 control
  **0.0927 [0.0802, 0.1058]**; count-matched random dots **0.0458**. The lane loses to *random*.
- **Physics of the failure [MEASURED]:** at 100 m aggregation the LiDAR bands are max-pooled
  over 2,500 sub-cells, so every fan channel and gully saturates them: catalogue-vs-far-field
  contrast ≤1.42×, best single-field AUC 0.631, fitted-model holdout AUC 0.620. The top of the
  ranking hits within-300 m of withheld truth only 3.0% of the time (random: 1.7%), and because
  the metric pays for *distinct* truth pixels covered, concentrated-but-imprecise dots lose to a
  uniform spread. DEM10 one-sided step fields are no better (AUC 0.496–0.606).
- **Lesson:** per-pixel 100 m fields cap at AUC ≈ 0.63 no matter the sensor. The information that
  separates 0.28 from 0.44 live is *spatial context + multi-physics corroboration learned OOF* —
  exactly the H19-5/P9 recipe family. A new lane must add information that recipe lacks
  (e.g., 2-D patch context at 1–10 m, or a genuinely new data type), not re-derive scarps.

## 5. Can 0.2778 be beaten, and what would it take?

Score algebra at the incumbent's operating point: with coverage c = TP_w/|G| and
ρ = FP_w/|G|, c = 0.2·s·(ρ+4)/(1−0.2s). At s=0.2778, c≈0.27–0.33 for ρ∈[1,2]; the public leader
(0.4413, **[OFFICIAL leaderboard, read 2026-10-21]**) implies c≈0.48–0.58 — i.e., the leader's
surface *finds roughly twice as much of the hidden truth*. Emission refinements around the
incumbent are worth at most ~+0.01 (the whole B2 prune was +0.007 user-reported); the remaining
gap is surface quality. The credible routes, in order:

1. **2-D spatial-context models** (patch CNN/U-Net) over the 19-band stack + LiDAR derivatives,
   trained whole-component-OOF with no catalogue-distance inputs — the organizer's own reference
   architecture, which none of the ~80 family lanes has actually run at full scale (compute).
2. **Better calibration of selection:** local instruments barely track live
   (**[MEASURED]** this session, 77 hash-verified rasters: Spearman vs user-reported live —
   Instrument A-as-is ρ=0.16 n.s., SGMC B′ ρ=0.33, piedmont-conditioned C76 ρ=0.52; B′ is
   trivially gameable, ρ inflated by SGMC-tainted files). Selector decisions should weight
   C76-pied + fold-honest A jointly and distrust any single number.
3. **Budget/spacing re-optimisation on a winning surface** via exact max-cover with an honest
   |G| prior (lattice inversion gives |G_live| ≈ 12,300 px, independently recomputed here;
   GEMSDOE70 reported ≈12,590 by a similar route).

**What this session ships instead of a slot candidate:** a fully validated, provably unique
(mutual overlap ≤0.106 vs 122 registry rasters) but **negative** artifact, labelled
DO-NOT-SUBMIT in its filename, plus the instrument audit above so the human selector can choose
among *existing* unscored files (notably GEMSDOE70's P9 hero: C76-pied 0.1079, the highest of any
unscored 37,654-dot file measured here — a measurement, not a pick; slot choice is the separate
selector step and no slot was used by this session).
